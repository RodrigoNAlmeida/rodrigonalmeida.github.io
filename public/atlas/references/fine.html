Sources and background

Fine general frame

Explore this construction →
Motivation

K. Fine introduced this frame to study incompleteness of S4 logics. V. Shehtman later adapted it to obtain an incomplete superintuitionistic logic. It has served as a useful source of counterexamples: for instance, G. Bezhanishvili, D. Gabelaia, and M. Jibladze employed it in their negative solution to Kuznetsov’s problem for bi-Heyting algebras.

Details of the construction

The Fine poset consists of four countable subsets: A,B,C,D, labelled accordingly as ai,bi,ci,di, etc. They have the following roles: the B and C parts are descending ladder-like constructions (reminiscent of the Mardaev frame and Rieger-Nishimura); the A part forms an antichain, which covers progressively deeper parts of B∪C; and D forms an infinite ascending chain, with designated points below A, and all of them below B∪C. In detail, the cover relations are:

bi+1 < bi,   ci+1 < ci
bi+2 < ci,   ci+2 < bi
ai < bi+1, ci+1
di < ai, di+1

The Fine general frame adds the point ∞, in the center, which compactifies all three of the above components, and sits between D and B∪C.

di < ∞ < bj, cj
p = {c₀},   q = {b₀}
↑di = {dj, aj : j ≥ i} ∪ B ∪ C ∪ {∞}

The algebra is 2-generated, being generated by the two maximal singletons.

The algebra enjoys many interesting features: it is a so-called KM-algebra, a bi-Heyting algebra, and validates the De Jongh-Gabbay axiom bb2. This latter axiom is important in Shehtman's incompleteness proof and the Bezhanishvili-Gabelaia-Jibladze proof, since it is what makes candidate Kripke frames or complete bi-Heyting algebras validate axioms which the Fine general frame does not.
