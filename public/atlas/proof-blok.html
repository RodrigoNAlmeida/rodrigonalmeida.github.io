1. Blok-Esakia Theorem

A modal companion of a superintuitionistic (si) logic LL is any normal modal logic above S4S4 in which LL fully and faithfully embeds via the Gödel translation. The notion of a modal companion has a rich theory [6, 8, 10, 7, 4, 12, 3], culminating in a result known as the Blok-Esakia theorem [7, 2]. The latter states that the lattice si logics is completely isomorphic to the lattice of normal extensions of GrzGrz, via the mapping that sends a si logic LL to the least normal extension of GrzGrz containing the Gödel translations of all theorems of LL.

Source textPage 1

The literature contains several proofs of the Blok-Esakia theorem. Blok’s original proof [2] is algebraic and notoriously involved (see also [11]). Esakia appears to have given a dual proof, which remains unpublished. Zacharyaschev later gave a proof using the machinery of canonical formulas [13], which Jeřábek [9] later extended to rule systems using canonical rules. More recently, Bezhanishvili and Cleani [1] (see also [5]) offered an alternative proof based on stable canonical rules, which are algebra-based rules that are to filtration what Jerabek’s canonical rules are to selective filtration.

Our contribution is to show that the proof by Bezhanishvili and Cleani can be carried out without the machinery of stable canonical rules. Moreover, the key idea of that proof can be adapted to obtain a dual, order-topological proof that resembles Blok’s original algebraic one, and gives some intuitions on it.

2. Blok’s Lemma

We begin with some preliminary definitions. Let HH be a Heyting algebra. The modal algebra σH\sigma H is constructed by expanding the free Boolean extension B(H)B(H) of HH with the operator

□a:=⋁{b∈H:b≤a}.\square a := \bigvee\{b\in H:b\leq a\}.

It is well known that σH\sigma H is always a GrzGrz-algebra.

Conversely, given an S4S4-algebra MM, the skeleton ρM\rho M of MM is simply the Heyting algebra of open elements of MM. We recall that an element aa of MM is open when □a=a\square a=a, and that the Heyting implication of ρM\rho M is given by a→b:=□(a→b)a\to b:=\square(a\to b).

It is well-known that ρ\rho commutes with all variety operations. Sigma commutes with subalgebras and homomorphic images, and also finite products, but not with arbitrary products in general.

Dually, when XX is an Esakia space we let σX:=X\sigma X:=X. Conversely, when YY is an S4S4-space, we let ρY\rho Y be the Esakia space that results from YY by collapsing all clusters, and endowing the result with the quotient topology under the cluster collapse mapping. The algebraic and topological versions of the mappings σ,ρ\sigma,\rho are dual to one another.

A GrzGrz-algebra MM is called skeletal when it is isomorphic to σρM\sigma\rho M. Blok derives the Blok-Esakia theorem as a consequence of the following Lemma, now widely known as Blok’s Lemma.

Lemma 1 (Blok’s Lemma). Let MM be a GrzGrz-algebra. Then M∈ISPU(σρM)M\in\mathrm{IS}\mathrm{P}_{\mathrm{U}}(\sigma\rho M).

Our goal in this work is to provide a different, more geometric, proof of the same fact.

Source textPage 2

Let M,NM,N be modal algebras and let A,BA,B be Boolean subalgebras of M,NM,N respectively. A mapping h:A→Bh:A\to B is called a □\square-homomorphism when it is a Boolean homomorphism and h(□a)=□h(a)h(\square a)=\square h(a) whenever □a∈A\square a\in A. The key step in Blok’s proof of Lemma 1 is the following result.

Lemma 2 (Algebraic embedding lemma). Let MM be a GrzGrz-algebra and let NN be a finite Boolean subalgebra of MM. Then there is a □\square-embedding h:N→σρMh:N\to\sigma\rho M.

Source textPage 2

The proof given in [1], in turn, makes use of stable maps between modal spaces:

Definition 3. Given modal spaces X=(X,R)X=(X,R) and Y=(Y,R)Y=(Y,R), a continuous function f:X→Yf:X\to Y is said to be stable if f(x)Rf(y)f(x)Rf(y) holds whenever xRyxRy. Given D⊆Clop(Y)D\subseteq\mathrm{Clop}(Y) we say that ff satisfies the bounded domain condition (BDC) with respect to DD if for all U∈DU\in D, if f(x)Ryf(x)Ry and y∈Uy\in U, then there is some x′x' such that xRx′xRx' and f(x′)∈Uf(x')\in U.

Given YY a finite S4S4-space, and DD a domain, 𝒥(Y,D)\mathcal{J}(Y,D) denotes the stable canonical rule. Bezhanishvili and Cleani show that for a GrzGrz-space XX, X⊮𝒥(Y,D)X\nVdash\mathcal{J}(Y,D) implies ρX⊮𝒥(Y,D)\rho X\nVdash\mathcal{J}(Y,D), which amounts to showing the following lemma:

Lemma 4. Given a GrzGrz-space X=(X,R)X=(X,R), a finite S4S4-space Y=(Y,R)Y=(Y,R), and a surjective stable map p:X→Yp:X\to Y satisfying the BDC for a domain DD, there is a stable surjection p′:ρX→Yp':\rho X\to Y satisfying the BDC for the domain DD.

Source textPage 2

One can then show the following:

Proposition 5. The following statements are equivalent:

  1. Blok’s Lemma;
  2. The algebraic stable embedding lemma;
  3. The stable surjection lemma.
Proof

The equivalence of (2-3) follows strictly from duality. Given the algebraic stable embedding lemma, one can obtain Blok’s lemma by model theoretic reasoning: to show that MM embeds into an ultrapower of σρ(M)\sigma\rho(M), pick a finite collection of elements from MM. Form a finite Boolean subalgebra of MM with these elements, say NN; note that NN then is a partial modal algebra. Using the algebraic stable embedding lemma, we find a □\Box-preserving injection to σρ(M)\sigma\rho(M). This ensures that the finite diagram can be realized. By classical model theory results, this shows that MM embeds into an ultrapower of σρ(M)\sigma\rho(M).

For the converse, assume Blok’s lemma. It is known that satisfying the same universal class means that each finite diagram embeds into an algebra in the target, which yields the algebraic stable embedding lemma.

3. Step-by-Step Proofs of Blok’s Lemma

By considering the specifics of the algebraic proof of Blok’s lemma and the order-topological one of [1], we will obtain a new dual proof which closely mirrors the original one of Blok. This starts with the following:

Lemma 6 (Algebraic one-step embedding). Let MM be a GrzGrz-algebra and let NN be a finite Boolean subalgebra, such that h:N→σρMh:N\to\sigma\rho M is a □\square-embedding fixing all open elements. If x∈Mx\in M is arbitrary, then there are finitely many open elements CC, and a □\square-embedding h′:⟨N∪{x}∪C⟩→σρMh':\langle N\cup\{x\}\cup C\rangle\to\sigma\rho M such that h′↾N=hh'\upharpoonright_N=h.

Indeed, having this lemma, and an arbitrary finite Boolean subalgebra NN, one enumerates N=Nop∪{x1,...,xn}N=N_{op}\cup\{x_1,...,x_n\} where Nop=⟨{□a:□a∈N}⟩N_{op}=\langle\{\square a:\square a\in N\}\rangle. Then one sets N0=NopN_0=N_{op}, and successively adds one element, creating a sequence of algebras:

N0→N1→...→NnN_0\to N_1\to...\to N_n

where N⊆NnN\subseteq N_n. Having a □\square-embedding of the latter algebra gives us a □\square-embedding of NN. It is the way that such elements are picked which demands the Grz-axiom: given NkN_k, to extend the embedding to ⟨Nk∪{xk+1}⟩\langle N_k\cup\{x_{k+1}\}\rangle one needs only define the image of xk+1x_{k+1}. This is done by picking, for each c∈Nkc\in N_k, an element element wcw_c which is defined, by letting uc=¬xk+1∨cu_c=\neg x_{k+1}\lor c as:

wc:=¬(□(u→□u)→□u).w_c:=\neg(\square(u\to\square u)\to\square u).

The use of the Grz-axiom lies in ensuring that □(□(u→□u)→□u)≤u\square(\square(u\to\square u)\to\square u)\leq u; dually, this follows if:

u≤◇πuu\leq\Diamond\pi u

where πu=u∧¬◇(◇u∧¬u)\pi u=u\land\neg\Diamond(\Diamond u\land\neg u). This is called by Esakia the rest of uu; dually, given a clopen UU, πU\pi U is called the set of passive points of UU, and it is known that the Grz-axiom corresponds to every point in UU being below a passive point.

Applying the key idea of the proof of [1, Main Lemma], one can obtain a dual step-by-step proof of Blok’s lemma. Given a space XX, let ρ:X→ρX\rho:X\to\rho X be the cluster collapse continuous map. Moreover, a surjection 𝜚:X→Y\varrho:X\to Y is called a cluster-reducing map when it is the quotient map induced by some equivalence relation on YY that never relates points belonging to different clusters in XX.

Source textPage 3

Lemma 7 (Dual one-step surjection). Let X=(X,R),Y=(Y,R)X=(X,R),Y=(Y,R) and Y′=(Y′,R)Y'=(Y',R) be S4S4 spaces with the following properties.

  • X=(X,R)X=(X,R) is a Grz space;
  • Y′=Y⊔{•}Y'=Y\sqcup\{\bullet\} and there is a cluster-reducing map 𝜚:Y′→Y\varrho:Y'\to Y which identifies •\bullet with some point in its cluster, but does not identify any other points;
  • There is a stable surjection f:X→Y′f:X\to Y' satisfying the BDC for some D⊆Y′D\subseteq Y';
  • There is a stable surjection g:σρX→Yg:\sigma\rho X\to Y satisfying the BDC for 𝜚[D]\varrho[D].

Then there is a stable map h:σρX→Y′h:\sigma\rho X\to Y' satisfying the BDC for DD.

Source textPage 3

Note max(f−1[•])\max(f^{-1}[\bullet]) and max(f−1[x])\max(f^{-1}[x]) are disjoint and closed. Moreover, because XX is a Grz-space, both these sets consist entirely of passive elements.

Consequently, their images U•:=ρ[max(f−1[•])]U_{\bullet}:=\rho[\max(f^{-1}[\bullet])] and Ux:=ρ[max(f−1[x])]U_x:=\rho[\max(f^{-1}[x])] are closed in σρX\sigma\rho X.

Now, U•U_{\bullet} and UxU_x are contained in the clopen U:=ρ(f−1{x,•})U:=\rho(f^{-1}\{x,\bullet\}), thus we can partition UU in two clopens Vx⊇UxV_x\supseteq U_x and V•⊇U•V_{\bullet}\supseteq U_{\bullet}.

We then put h−1(x)=Vxh^{-1}(x)=V_x and h−1(•)=V•h^{-1}(\bullet)=V_{\bullet}. So hh is a stable surjection that satisfies the BDC for DD, as desired.

For a more detailed description of this procedure see

N. Bezhanishvili and A. M. Cleani, Blok–Esakia theorems via stable canonical rules, The Journal of Symbolic Logic 91(1) (2026), 68–104.

Having obtained Lemma 7, we prove Blok’s lemma following the algebraic proof strategy. Given a finite S4S4-space Y=(Y,R)Y=(Y,R), we set Y0=σρYY_0=\sigma\rho Y and form the inverse chain

Y0←Y1←...←YnY_0\leftarrow Y_1\leftarrow...\leftarrow Y_n

where Yn=YY_n=Y, and Yi+1Y_{i+1} is obtained as some cluster-expansion of YiY_i by one additional element; the existence of the surjections in the chain being guaranteed by Lemma 7. The algebra N0N_0 is the dual of Y0Y_0, and the posets YkY_k – whilst not being isomorphic to the dual of NkN_k – can be seen as refinements of the decomposition given by the algebraic construction.
