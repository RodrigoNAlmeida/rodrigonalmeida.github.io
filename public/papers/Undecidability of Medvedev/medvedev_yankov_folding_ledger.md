#### Positive B, round-2 obstruction report (branch closed for reassignment)

For a witness \(f:M_I\to P\), a coordinate \(i\notin B\) has a first link over a
basis \(B\),
\[
  \ell_i^B(S)=f(S\cup\{i\})\qquad(S\subseteq B),
\]
and higher links record the same table after adjoining tuples of outside
coordinates.  If both \(|B|\) and the required link arity were bounded in terms
of \(P\), there would be only finitely many roles, and a Ramsey/pumping argument
could bound their multiplicities.

The argument stops before that hypothesis.  Bounded bisimulation controls the
**vertical** successor depth of a Kripke model; it gives no bound on the
**horizontal** arity of a simplex face or on the size of a context basis.
The diamond in B9 proves that arity-one/singleton roles do not suffice.  More
generally, B8 shows that even with fixed target \(C_2\), links of arbitrary arity
can contain an arbitrary abstract simplicial complex.  In particular, one may
color all proper faces of an arbitrarily large simplex by \(t\) and only its
full face by \(r\).  This is a valid witness, but no bounded subset of that
particular witness carries the root; the small \(M_2\) witness is obtained only
by a genuine recoloring/normalization.  Thus neither restriction, ordinary
type contraction, nor Ramsey homogenization of a given witness supplies the
needed basis.

The exact missing lemma is the following **bounded horizontal normal-form
lemma**: there are computable \(b(P),d(P),\mu(P)\) such that, whenever a witness
exists, one exists with a basis \(|B|\le b(P)\), with every outside coordinate
classified by its links of arity at most \(d(P)\), and with no link role used
more than \(\mu(P)\) times; moreover this classification is a congruence for
all disjoint-face unions.  This lemma immediately gives a computable Medvedev
dimension cutoff.  Conversely, a dimension cutoff gives it trivially by taking
all coordinates into \(B\).  Hence it is an exact normal-form version of finite
folding, not a consequence of Hennessy--Milner.

**Status:** B1, B2, B4, B5, B8, and B9 are settled contributions.  The latent-role
policy B10 has reached the same open point for a second round and is now
**closed/retired**.  The most precise future direction is to prove the bounded
horizontal normal-form lemma fiber-by-fiber for the maximal-support map of
Proposition 12.4, beginning with a normalization theorem that deletes arbitrary
simplicial-complex noise inside singleton-support fibers while preserving the
finite family of cross-fiber link roles.

### Positive A2: full proofs and round-3 synchronization analysis

Throughout this subsection, \(M_I=(\wp(I)\setminus\{\varnothing\},\supseteq)\), so
\(A\leq B\) means \(B\subseteq A\).  Put
\(K=\operatorname{Max}(P)\), let \(c(i)=f(\{i\})\in K\), and write
\(\operatorname{col}(A)=\{c(i):i\in A\}\).  Proposition 12.4 says
\(T(f(A))=\operatorname{col}(A)\).

#### A2-01 and A2-02: finite-visible core and local shadow

**A2-01 (proved; proof supplied to the auditor).**  Let
\[
 R_f=\{f(A):\varnothing\ne A\subseteq I\text{ is finite}\}.
\]
Then \(R_f\) is a principal upset \(\uparrow s\), and it is a p-morphic image of
one finite Medvedev frame.

Indeed, if \(f(A)=r\in R_f\) and \(r\leq q\), Back gives a nonempty
\(B\subseteq A\) with \(f(B)=q\), so \(q\in R_f\).  Choose a finite
representative \(A_r\) for every \(r\in R_f\), and put
\(J=\bigcup_{r\in R_f}A_r\).  The restriction \(f|_{M_J}\) maps into \(R_f\),
is onto by the choice of representatives, and retains Back because every
witness below a face \(A\subseteq J\) is itself a subset of \(J\).  Finally,
\(s=f(J)\leq f(A_r)=r\) for all \(r\in R_f\); hence \(s\) is the least point of
the upset \(R_f\), and \(R_f=\uparrow s\).

**A2-02 (proved; proof supplied to the auditor).**  For every nonempty
\(A\subseteq I\), let
\[
 R_A=\{f(B):\varnothing\ne B\subseteq A\text{ is finite}\}.
\]
There is a unique point \(\sigma(A)\) such that
\(R_A=\uparrow\sigma(A)\).  It has all of the following properties.

1. A finite \(B_0\subseteq A\) satisfies \(f(B_0)=\sigma(A)\).
2. \(f(A)\leq\sigma(A)\) and \(T(f(A))=T(\sigma(A))\).
3. For every finite \(C\subseteq A\), including \(C=\varnothing\),
   \(f(B_0\cup C)=\sigma(A)\).
4. \(\sigma:M_I\twoheadrightarrow_p R_f\) is itself an onto p-morphism and
   agrees with \(f\) on finite faces.

For the proof, \(R_A\) is an upset by Back.  It is downward directed: if
\(x=f(B)\) and \(y=f(C)\), then \(f(B\cup C)\leq x,y\).  More explicitly,
choose one finite representative \(B_x\subseteq A\) for each of the finitely
many \(x\in R_A\), let \(D=\bigcup_xB_x\), and put \(q=f(D)\).  Then
\(q\in R_A\) and \(q\leq x\) for every \(x\in R_A\); thus \(q=\sigma(A)\) is
the unique least point and \(R_A=\uparrow q\).  Take \(B_0=D\).  Since
\(B_0\subseteq A\), monotonicity gives \(f(A)\leq q\).  One inclusion
\(T(q)\subseteq T(f(A))\) follows from \(B_0\subseteq A\).  Conversely, for
each \(i\in A\), \(f(\{i\})\in R_A=\uparrow q\), so every maximal colour in
\(A\) lies above \(q\); Proposition 12.4 gives the reverse inclusion.  If
\(C\subseteq A\) is finite, then \(f(B_0\cup C)\in R_A\), hence it is above
\(q\), while \(B_0\subseteq B_0\cup C\) makes it below \(f(B_0)=q\); this is
the stabilization equality.  Finally, if \(B\subseteq A\), then
\(\sigma(A)\leq\sigma(B)\).  If \(t\geq\sigma(A)\), the equality
\(R_A=\uparrow\sigma(A)\) supplies a finite \(B\subseteq A\) with
\(f(B)=t\), and finiteness gives \(\sigma(B)=f(B)=t\).  This proves Back for
\(\sigma\), and finite representatives prove onto-ness.

The interval \([f(A),\sigma(A)]\) need not be a unary chain.  Let
\(P=\{p,a,b,t\}\) be the diamond \(p<a,t\), \(p<b<t\), with \(a\parallel b\),
and split \(I=E\sqcup F\) into two infinite sets.  Define \(f(A)=t\) when
\(A\) is finite; \(f(A)=a\) when \(A\cap E\) is infinite and \(A\cap F\) is
finite; symmetrically put \(f(A)=b\); and put \(f(A)=p\) when both parts are
infinite.  A direct four-case check gives an onto p-morphism.  For
\(f(A)=p\), \(\sigma(A)=t\), so the same-support shadow interval branches.

#### A2-04: exact disjoint-block slice cube

**A2-04 (proved).**  Suppose \(A_1,\ldots,A_\ell\subseteq I\) have pairwise
disjoint, nonempty colour supports \(U_i=T(f(A_i))\).  (The sets \(A_i\) are
then automatically disjoint.)  For \(\varnothing\ne S\subseteq[\ell]\), put
\[
 A_S=\bigcup_{i\in S}A_i,\qquad y_S=f(A_S),\qquad
 U_S=\bigcup_{i\in S}U_i.
\]
Then \(T(y_S)=U_S\), and for all nonempty \(R\subseteq S\),
\[
 \{q\geq y_S:T(q)\subseteq U_R\}=\uparrow y_R. \tag{DBS}
\]
For the nontrivial inclusion, Back at \(A_S\) represents \(q\) on a subface
\(B\subseteq A_S\).  If \(T(q)\subseteq U_R\), disjointness of the colour
supports forces \(B\subseteq A_R\), hence \(q\geq y_R\).  Conversely, Back
inside \(A_R\) represents every \(q\geq y_R\) on a subface of \(A_R\), and
such a \(q\) automatically has support contained in \(U_R\).

Consequently, for every tuple \(x_i\in P_{U_i}\) with pairwise disjoint
supports, arbitrary-base feasibility forces the existence of a finite cube
\((y_S)_{\varnothing\ne S\subseteq[\ell]}\), with \(y_{\{i\}}=x_i\), satisfying
(DBS).  This is a target-intrinsic finite CSP and hence decidable for fixed
\(P\).

**A2-05 (proved corollary: one-foreign-block law).**  Let \(f(A)=x\),
\(V=T(x)\), choose \(\varnothing\ne D\subseteq K\setminus V\), and let
\(E\) contain exactly one coordinate of each colour in \(D\).  If
\(y=f(A\cup E)\) and \(d=f(E)\), then
\[

#### A2-06: crown-replacement graph and higher coherence

For \(\varnothing\ne U\subseteq K\), let

\(\Gamma_U(P)\) is connected.  Indeed, the p-map colours the complete

#### A2-08: the support-fiber p-morphism diagram

**A2-08 (proved necessary; exact structural reduction).**  Let
\(f:M_I\twoheadrightarrow_pP\), let \(K=\operatorname{Max}(P)\), and write
\[
 I_m=\{i\in I:f(\{i\})=m\},\qquad
 P_U=\{p\in P:T(p)=U\}
\]
for \(m\in K\) and \(\varnothing\ne U\subseteq K\).  If
\(\varnothing\ne V\subseteq U\) and \(p\in P_U\), then
\(\uparrow p\cap P_V\) has a unique least point.  Denote it by
\[
 \delta_V^U(p)=\min(\uparrow p\cap P_V).
\]
Concretely, for **every** face \(A\) with \(f(A)=p\),
\[
 \delta_V^U(p)=f(A\cap I_V),
 \qquad I_V=\bigcup_{m\in V}I_m.                    \tag{D1}
\]
The maps \(\delta_V^U:P_U\twoheadrightarrow_pP_V\) are onto p-morphisms,
they obey
\[
 \delta_U^U=\operatorname{id}_{P_U},\qquad
 \delta_W^V\circ\delta_V^U=\delta_W^U
 \quad(\varnothing\ne W\subseteq V\subseteq U),   \tag{D2}
\]
and the entire cross-fiber order of \(P\) is recovered from them:
\[
 p\le q\quad(p\in P_U,q\in P_V)
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 V\subseteq U\text{ and }\delta_V^U(p)\le q.       \tag{D3}
\]
In particular every fiber \(P_U\) is a finite rooted poset (its least point
is \(\delta_U^K(r)\), where \(r\) is the root of \(P\)), and \(P\) is the
Grothendieck poset of a finite contravariant diagram of onto p-morphisms over
the nonempty-subset frame \(M_K\).

Here is the proof.  Proposition 12.4 says that the colors of the coordinates
in any face \(A\) are exactly \(T(f(A))\).  Thus if \(f(A)=p\in P_U\), the
face \(A_V=A\cap I_V\) is nonempty, has exact support \(V\), and its label
\(d=f(A_V)\) lies in \(\uparrow p\cap P_V\).  If
\(q\in\uparrow p\cap P_V\), Back at \(A\) gives a face \(B\subseteq A\)
with \(f(B)=q\).  Exact support forces \(B\subseteq A_V\), whence
\(d\le q\).  Therefore \(d\) is the unique minimum of
\(\uparrow p\cap P_V\), proving both principality and the independence of
the chosen preimage in (D1).

Functoriality is now literal coordinate restriction:
\((A\cap I_V)\cap I_W=A\cap I_W\), which proves (D2).  For monotonicity,
if \(p\le p'\) in \(P_U\), then
\(\delta_V^U(p')\in\uparrow p\cap P_V\), so minimality gives
\(\delta_V^U(p)\le\delta_V^U(p')\).  For Back, suppose
\(\delta_V^U(p)\le q\in P_V\), choose \(f(A)=p\), and use Back inside
\(A_V\) to obtain \(B\subseteq A_V\) with \(f(B)=q\).  Put
\[
 C=B\cup(A\cap I_{U\setminus V}),\qquad p'=f(C).
\]
Then \(C\subseteq A\), so \(p'\ge p\); exact support gives \(p'\in P_U\);
and (D1), applied to \(C\), gives \(\delta_V^U(p')=f(B)=q\).  Thus
\(\delta_V^U\) has Back.  Given arbitrary \(q\in P_V\), choose a preimage
face \(B\), adjoin one coordinate of each color in \(U\setminus V\), and
apply (D1); this proves onto-ness.  Finally, if \(p\le q\), support
monotonicity gives \(V\subseteq U\), and minimality gives
\(\delta_V^U(p)\le q\).  Conversely
\(p\le\delta_V^U(p)\le q\), proving (D3).

#### A2-09: the Segal axis condition

**A2-09 (proved necessary; headline finite interface).**  For every partition
\(U=U_1\sqcup\cdots\sqcup U_\ell\) into nonempty blocks, the joint axis map
\[
 \Delta_{(U_i)}:P_U\longrightarrow\prod_{i=1}^{\ell}P_{U_i},
 \qquad p\longmapsto(\delta_{U_i}^U(p))_{i=1}^{\ell}               \tag{D-S}
\]
is an onto p-morphism.

For (D-S), take arbitrary \(x_i\in P_{U_i}\), choose faces \(A_i\) labelled
\(x_i\), and union them.  Their supports are disjoint, hence so are the
faces, and (D1) says that the union label maps to the prescribed tuple;
thus \(\Delta_{(U_i)}\) is onto.  For Back, let \(f(A)=p\in P_U\) and suppose
\(q_i\ge\delta_{U_i}^U(p)\) for every \(i\).  Back inside the disjoint face
\(A\cap I_{U_i}\) supplies \(B_i\subseteq A\cap I_{U_i}\) labelled \(q_i\).
The face \(B=\bigcup_iB_i\) has label \(p'\in P_U\), satisfies \(p'\ge p\),
and (D1) gives \(\delta_{U_i}^U(p')=q_i\) simultaneously.  Monotonicity is
componentwise.  This proves the joint p-morphism claim.

The Segal condition subsumes A2-04.  Given disjoint supports \(U_i\), choose
\(y\in P_U\), \(U=\bigsqcup_iU_i\), mapping to their prescribed axis tuple and
put \(y_J=\delta_{\bigcup_{j\in J}U_j}^{U}(y)\).  Functoriality gives the
whole compatible cube, and (D3) turns each principal exact-support slice into
the full slice equation (DBS).  Conversely the disjoint-block construction
above is the concrete witness of onto-ness and Back for every such joint map.

Equivalently, arbitrary-base realizability is the existence of coordinate
blocks \((I_m)_{m\in K}\) and compatible onto p-morphisms
\[
 f_U:\prod_{m\in U}M_{I_m}\twoheadrightarrow_pP_U,
 \qquad
 \delta_V^U f_U=f_V\,r_V^U,                       \tag{D4}
\]
where \(r_V^U\) deletes the coordinate blocks outside \(V\).  Conversely,
any family (D4) glues to an onto p-morphism onto \(P\): Back within a support
fiber is supplied by \(f_U\), while Back to a smaller support is supplied by
\(f_V\) together with (D3).  Hence finite folding is exactly the problem of
simultaneously replacing the blocks in this finite diagram by finite blocks.

The diagram axioms are not sufficient.  Target 6:43 satisfies all of them:
its two singleton fibers are singletons and every full-support point maps to
the two maxima.  It nevertheless violates the replacement-connectivity law
A2-06.  On the other hand A2-08 gives a particularly clean certificate for
SH-11.  For the cover list
\[
 r<a,\ r<b,\ a<m,\ a<n,\ b<c,\ c<m,
\]
the full-support fiber is \(\{r<a\}\), the \(\{m\}\)-fiber is the chain
\(b<c<m\), and the forced map sends \(r\mapsto b\), \(a\mapsto m\).  It
misses \(c\) in the Back cone above \(b\), so it is not a p-morphism.

For two maximal colors, (D4) reduces the problem to one commutative square:
finite p-morphisms onto the two pure fibers and a compatible p-morphism from
their product onto the mixed fiber.  A2-06 controls changes of a single
coordinate only over the top pair of axis values.  The still-open sufficiency
problem is to decide whether diagrammatic Back plus replacement connectivity
at **every** axis pair (and every relative principal cone), rather than only
at the top pair, supplies a finite compatible realization.

#### A2-10: exact two-axis simultaneous-realization lemma

**A2-10 (proved equivalence).**  Suppose \(K=\{0,1\}\), put
\(P_i=P_{\{i\}}\), \(P_{01}=P_K\), and let
\(\Delta=(\delta_{\{0\}}^K,\delta_{\{1\}}^K):P_{01}\twoheadrightarrow_p
P_0\times P_1\).  For any nonempty coordinate blocks \(I_0,I_1\), there is
an onto p-morphism
\[
 M_{I_0\sqcup I_1}\twoheadrightarrow_pP                         \tag{2A-1}
\]
if and only if there are onto p-morphisms
\[
 g_i:M_{I_i}\twoheadrightarrow_pP_i\quad(i=0,1),\qquad
 h:M_{I_0}\times M_{I_1}\twoheadrightarrow_pP_{01}               \tag{2A-2}
\]
such that
\[
 \Delta\circ h=g_0\times g_1.                                    \tag{2A-3}
\]
The same equivalence holds with “finite” imposed on both coordinate blocks.

Indeed, restrict (2A-1) to the two pure-support strata and the mixed-support
stratum; (D1) gives (2A-3).  Conversely define the value of a pure face by
the corresponding \(g_i\), and of a mixed face \((A_0,A_1)\) by
\(h(A_0,A_1)\).  Forth and Back within one stratum come from \(g_i,h\).
For a mixed face labelled \(p\), a requested pure successor \(q\in P_i\)
satisfies \(\delta_i(p)\le q\) by (D3); Back for \(g_i\) below \(A_i\)
therefore supplies the required pure subface.  These are all cases.

Both \(P_0\) and \(P_1\) have a unique greatest point and hence admit finite
Medvedev covers by A3-01.  The unresolved clause is **simultaneous**, not
fiberwise: one must choose finite covers \(g_0,g_1\) for which the product
map lifts through \(\Delta\) to an onto p-map \(h\).  If \(\Delta\) is
injective, it is an isomorphism and the lift is simply
\(\Delta^{-1}(g_0\times g_1)\), proving finite folding in that case.  Thus,
already for two colors, the exact residual consists of the non-singleton
fibers
\[
 F_{a,b}=\Delta^{-1}(a,b)\qquad(a\in P_0,\ b\in P_1)
\]
together with the compatibility of their labels under changing, deleting,
and adjoining coordinates.  A2-06 is the first (one-coordinate-replacement,
top-axis) layer of this compatibility.  Higher multiplicity profiles and
their restrictions are the remaining data; individual p-map Back for
\(\Delta\), even together with all marginal slice laws, does not encode them
(target 6:43 is the minimal witness).

#### A2 round-3 obstruction report and finite sufficient fragment

The principal-slice diagram and its Segal maps are exact and decidable, but
their nontrivial fibers do not remember how two representatives with the same
axes can replace one another inside a larger face.  The replacement graphs
provide the first contextual layer, yet higher-dimensional unions require
compatible profiles on all coordinate-multiplicity vectors, not merely
pairwise graph adjacency.  This is the same horizontal-context obstruction
isolated in B10, now expressed precisely as simultaneous lifting through the
nontrivial Segal fibers (A2-10).

One finite sufficient construction is available.  For each maximal colour
\(m\), let \(P_m=T^{-1}(\{m\})\), adjoin a new greatest point \(*_m\), and put
\[
 G(P)=\left(\prod_{m\in K}(P_m\cup\{*_m\})\right)
       \setminus\{(*_m)_{m\in K}\}.
\]
If every \(P_m\) is a finite Medvedev image and there is a support-preserving
onto p-morphism \(G(P)\twoheadrightarrow_pP\), then \(P\) is a finite
Medvedev image: extend the maps onto the \(P_m\)'s by sending the empty face
to \(*_m\), take their finite product, identify the resulting punctured
product with one finite \(M_N\), and compose.  All objects are finite, so this
is a decidable sufficient test.  It is not necessary: target 4:3, with covers
`2<0, 2<1, 3<2`, folds from \(M_3\), while its singleton fibers make
\(G(P)=M_2\), which cannot cover the added unary root.  Closing this product
construction under SH-15 unary stems and under compatible same-support
fillings is the precise next positive direction.

**Branch status (round 3, closed after requested finalization).**  The
seven-point boundary is completely resolved.  The general
finite-folding theorem and a computable bound remain open.  The least missing
ingredient is a finite-index/pumping theorem for the hierarchy of
coordinate-replacement profiles \(\Gamma_U^{(r)}(P)\), where \(r\) records up
to \(r\) repeated coordinates in each maximal colour.  A proof that this
hierarchy stabilizes at a computable \(r=r(P)\), followed by the finite product
construction above, would decide finite folding.  No stabilization proof is
currently available.  The least possible unresolved target size is eight.

### Proof details for the audited structural lemmas

For **A2-01**, if \(f(A)=r\in R_f\), \(A\) is finite, and \(r\leq q\),
Back supplies a nonempty \(B\subseteq A\) with \(f(B)=q\); hence \(R_f\) is
an upset.  Choose a finite representative \(A_r\) for each \(r\in R_f\), and
put \(J=\bigcup_r A_r\).  Then \(f(J)\leq r\) for every \(r\in R_f\), so
\(R_f=\uparrow f(J)\).  If \(A\subseteq J\) and \(f(A)\leq q\in R_f\),
the original Back clause gives a nonempty \(B\subseteq A\subseteq J\) with
label \(q\).  Thus the restriction is an onto p-morphism.

For **A2-02**, let
\[
  R_A=\{f(B):\varnothing\neq B\subseteq A,\ B\text{ finite}\}.
\]
This is an upset and is downward directed: labels of \(B\) and \(C\) have the
common lower bound \(f(B\cup C)\) in \(R_A\).  Since \(P\) is finite, take one
finite representative for every member of \(R_A\) and union them.  Its label
is the least member \(\sigma(A)\), so \(R_A=\uparrow\sigma(A)\).  An attained
witness \(B_0\subseteq A\) gives \(f(A)\leq\sigma(A)\).  Proposition 12.4
first gives \(T(\sigma(A))\subseteq T(f(A))\); conversely every coordinate
color occurring in \(A\) labels a singleton in \(R_A=\uparrow\sigma(A)\), so
it is maximal above \(\sigma(A)\).  Hence the supports are equal.  For every
finite \(C\subseteq A\), minimality and monotonicity give
\[
  \sigma(A)\leq f(B_0\cup C)\leq f(B_0)=\sigma(A).
\]
Finally, if \(t\geq\sigma(A)\), then \(t\in R_A\), so some finite
\(B\subseteq A\) has \(f(B)=t=\sigma(B)\).  This is exactly Back for
\(\sigma\); order preservation and onto-ness are immediate.

For **SH-08**, define \(G_0=\operatorname{Max}(P)\), with cost one.  If
\(q_0,q_1\) are already generated and
\[
  \{z:p\leq z\leq q_0\text{ and }z\leq q_1\}=\{p\},
\]
adjoin \(p\), with cost at most \(\operatorname{cost}(q_0)+
\operatorname{cost}(q_1)\).  Given \(f(A)=p\), Back produces
\(A_j\subseteq A\) labeled \(q_j\); induction produces finite
\(E_j\subseteq A_j\) with the same labels.  For \(E=E_0\cup E_1\), Forth
gives
\[
  p=f(A)\leq f(E)\leq f(E_j)=q_j\quad(j=0,1),
\]
so separation forces \(f(E)=p\).  If \(p\) is the root, restriction to
\(M_E\) inherits Back and is onto by Back at \(E\).  In a duplicate-free
frame, two distinct immediate successors provide the required separating
pair.  Induction on vertex-depth therefore gives cost at most

hereditary family \(\mathcal H\) of nonempty subsets containing all singletons:

Here is the full construction and p-morphism check for **A3-01**.  In a finite
rooted poset a unique maximal point \(\top\) is a greatest point.  If
\(P=\{\top\}\), use the constant isomorphism from \(M_1\).  Otherwise put
\(Q=P\setminus\{\top\}\).  Let \(T\) be the cover-tree unravelling of \(Q\):
its nodes are saturated chains in \(Q\) beginning at the root, ordered by
prefix, and \(h:T\to Q\) sends a chain to its endpoint.  If \(u\preceq v\),
then \(h(u)\leq h(v)\).  Given \(q\geq h(u)\), extend the path \(u\) along a
saturated chain from \(h(u)\) to \(q\); this proves Back and onto-ness.  Thus
\(h\) is an onto p-morphism.  Adjoin one common greatest point \(\top\) to
\(T\), and send it to the greatest point of \(P\).  The resulting
\(h^+:T^+\twoheadrightarrow_pP\) is again an onto p-morphism.

It remains to realize \(T^+\).  More generally, for every node \(v\) of \(T\),
write \(T_v^+\) for the subtree rooted at \(v\), with the same common new top.
We recursively construct
\[
  \Phi_v:M_{D_v}\twoheadrightarrow_pT_v^+.
\]
If \(v\) is a leaf, take two coordinates and send their two singletons to
\(\top\) and the full two-face to \(v\).  This is the two-point-chain
p-morphism.

Suppose that \(v\) has children \(v_0,\ldots,v_k\), and recursively we have
\(\phi_i:M_{E_i}\twoheadrightarrow_pT_{v_i}^+\), with the coordinate sets
\(E_i\) pairwise disjoint.  Adjoin one fresh coordinate \(e_i\) to each block,
put \(B_i=E_i\cup\{e_i\}\), and let \(D=\bigsqcup_iB_i\).  First define, for
every nonempty \(X\subseteq B_i\),
\[
 g_i(X)=
 \begin{cases}
   v,&X=B_i,\\
   \phi_i(X\cap E_i),&X\neq B_i\text{ and }X\cap E_i\neq\varnothing,\\
   \top,&X=\{e_i\}.
 \end{cases}
\]
Thus \(g_i:M_{B_i}\twoheadrightarrow_p(\{v\}\cup T_{v_i}^+)\): on the
full face its proper subfaces realize all of \(T_{v_i}^+\), while on a proper
face meeting \(E_i\) the exact successor image is inherited from \(\phi_i\);
the remaining one-point face has the singleton successor cone \(\{\top\}\).

Call a nonempty face \(A\subseteq D\) *\(i\)-active* when
\[
 D\setminus B_i\subseteq A\quad\text{and}\quad A\cap B_i\neq\varnothing.
\]
Define the global coloring by
\[
 \Phi_v(A)=
 \begin{cases}
   g_i(A\cap B_i),&A\text{ is }i\text{-active for some }i,\\
   \top,&A\text{ is inactive for every }i.
 \end{cases}                                                    \tag{A3.1}
\]
This is well-defined: if distinct \(i,j\) are both active, then \(A\) contains
both \(D\setminus B_i\) and \(D\setminus B_j\); disjointness of the blocks
forces \(A=D\), and every possible value is then \(g_i(B_i)=v\).

Surjectivity is explicit.  The full face \(D\) maps to \(v\).  If
\(x\in T_{v_i}^+\), choose nonempty \(X\subseteq B_i\) with \(g_i(X)=x\) and
\(X\neq B_i\), and use
\(A=X\cup(D\setminus B_i)\); this face is uniquely \(i\)-active and maps to
\(x\).  (For the common top take, for example, \(X=\{e_i\}\).)

For Forth, suppose \(A\supseteq A'\), which is \(A\leq A'\) in the Medvedev
order.  If \(A'\) is inactive, then \(\Phi_v(A')=\top\), so the conclusion is
automatic.  If \(A'\) is \(i\)-active, then \(A\) is also \(i\)-active.  If
\(A\neq D\), apply Forth for \(g_i\) to
\(A\cap B_i\supseteq A'\cap B_i\); if \(A=D\), its value \(v\) is below
everything in \(T_v^+\).

For Back, let \(x\geq\Phi_v(A)\).  There are three cases.

1. If \(A=D\), then \(\Phi_v(A)=v\), and the already proved surjectivity gives
   a nonempty \(A'\subseteq D=A\) with \(\Phi_v(A')=x\).
2. If \(A\) is inactive, every subface of \(A\) is inactive as well; its value
   is \(\top\), so necessarily \(x=\top\) and one may take \(A'=A\).  This is
   the case that handles a face meeting only some of three or more root
   branches: it and its entire subface cone are deliberately normalized to
   the common top.
3. If \(A\neq D\) is active, it is active for a unique \(i\), and
   \(X=A\cap B_i\) is a nonempty proper face.  Back for \(g_i\) gives a
   nonempty \(Y\subseteq X\) with \(g_i(Y)=x\).  Then
   \(A'=Y\cup(D\setminus B_i)\subseteq A\) is uniquely \(i\)-active and
   \(\Phi_v(A')=x\).

Hence \(\Phi_v\) is an onto p-morphism, completing the induction.  Composition
with \(h^+\) gives the promised map onto \(P\).

Finally, if \(n_v=|T_v|\) and \(\ell_v\) is its number of leaves, the
construction uses
\[
 N(v)=2\quad(v\text{ a leaf}),\qquad
 N(v)=\sum_{v_i\text{ child of }v}\bigl(N(v_i)+1\bigr).
\]
Induction gives \(N(v)=n_v-1+2\ell_v\).  Every node of the cover-tree is a
chain in the \((s-1)\)-point poset \(Q\) containing its root, and its underlying
set determines the chain, so \(|T|\leq2^{s-2}\); also
\(|\operatorname{Leaf}(T)|\leq|T|\).  Therefore
\[
 N(T)=|T|-1+2|\operatorname{Leaf}(T)|
 \leq 3\cdot2^{s-2}-1.
\]
The formula was independently exercised against the exact face-profile
checker on every unique-maximum rooted target with at most five points; all
constructed colorings satisfy the p-morphism equations.  This computation is
only a regression check, not part of the proof.

**Independent audit A3-AUD.**  The auditor separately checked the local maps
\(g_i\), trigger well-definedness, Forth and all three Back cases.  In
particular, a proper face with a unique trigger cannot gain a different trigger
on a subface, while the full face realizes every child cone by varying one
block and keeping all other blocks full.  Separate exhaustive checks on the
three- and four-coatom stars (construction dimensions 9 and 12) satisfied the
exact face equation on all \(2^N-1\) faces.  The recurrence and the bound were
also independently verified.  **Status: audit passed; A3-01--03 are Proved.**

This tree-block construction is the constructive argument appearing as Claim
9 in Gaëlle Fontaine, *Axiomatization of ML and Cheq* (2006), pp. 12--14; the
explicit recurrence and cardinality-only estimate above are extracted from
that construction for the present folding problem.

The single extension suggested by A3-04 is therefore a **boundary-relative

color in \(K\setminus U\), and require the block construction to preserve
these states under disjoint union.  SH-11 shows that merely realizing each
fiber and its clean crown loses exactly this boundary information.

### VC-01: the collapsed visible core is not the obstruction

Let (f:M_I\twoheadrightarrow_pP), and let (R=R_f=\uparrow s) be the
finite-visible core from A2-01.  Collapse (R) to one point (\top), obtaining

\[
 Q=(P\setminus R)\cup\{\top\}.
\]

The quotient map (q:P\twoheadrightarrow_pQ) is a p-morphism.  The point
(\top) is greatest: every point of (P) lies below a maximal point, and all
maximal points belong to (R).  For a point outside (R), Back for (q) is
just the definition of the quotient order; for a point in the upset (R), its
whole cone is collapsed to (\top).

By the classical Maksimova--Skvortsov--Shehtman lemma, every finite rooted
frame with a greatest point is a p-morphic image of a finite Medvedev frame
(see also Xiao 2026, Lemma 2.4).  Hence (Q) always has a finite realization.
Together with A2-01, this proves that both pieces (Q) and (R) fold.  What is
not automatic is their amalgamation: the missing datum is the interface

\[
 \Lambda(u)=\uparrow_Pu\cap R\qquad(u\in P\setminus R).
\]

**Status:** **Proved/cited.**  This rules out spending further effort on the
uncoloured quotient (Q); all remaining work is relative/interface folding.

### VC-02: audited uniform top-substitution theorem

Let (Q) be finite and rooted with greatest point (\top), let (R) be
finite and rooted with root (\rho), and suppose

\[
 g:M_A\twoheadrightarrow_pQ,
 \qquad h:M_B\twoheadrightarrow_pR
\]

with (A,B) finite and nonempty.  Form the linear sum

\[
 P=(Q\setminus\{\top\})\oplus R,
\]

so every point of (Q\setminus\{\top\}) lies below every point of (R).
Then (M_{A\times B}\twoheadrightarrow_pP).  In particular the required
source dimension is at most (|A||B|).

For (\varnothing\ne S\subseteq A\times B), put

\[
 S_a=\{b:(a,b)\in S\},\qquad
 Y(S)=\bigcup_aS_a,\qquad
 X(S)=\{a:S_a\ne\varnothing\text{ and }h(S_a)=\rho\}.
\]

Define

\[
 F(S)=
 \begin{cases}
   g(X(S)),&X(S)\ne\varnothing\text{ and }g(X(S))\ne\top,\\
   h(Y(S)),&\text{otherwise}.
 \end{cases}
\]

If (T\subseteq S), then (Y(T)\subseteq Y(S)) and (X(T)\subseteq X(S)):
indeed, (h(T_a)=\rho) implies
(h(S_a)\le h(T_a)=\rho), hence (h(S_a)=\rho).  This proves Forth.
If (F(S)=u\in Q\setminus\{\top\}), then a successor
(v\in Q\setminus\{\top\}) is obtained by retaining precisely the root
fibres indexed by a set (Z\subseteq X(S)) with (g(Z)=v).  A successor
(r\in R) is obtained inside one root fibre (S_a), using Back for (h).
When (r=\rho), the remaining index set is the singleton (\{a\}), and
(g(\{a\})=\top) because (Q) has a unique maximal point; hence this face
does enter the (R)-branch of the definition.

If (F(S)\in R), all subfaces remain in the (R)-branch.  This is immediate
when (X(S)=\varnothing); if (g(X(S))=\top), then every nonempty
(X(T)\subseteq X(S)) also has (g(X(T))=\top).  For (r'\ge h(Y(S))), take
(V\subseteq Y(S)) with (h(V)=r') and put
(T=S\cap(A\times V)); then (Y(T)=V).  This proves Back.  Onto-ness follows
from the faces (Z\times B) for the (Q)-part and (\{a\}\times V) for the
(R)-part.

Applied to the visible core, this proves folding whenever

\[
 \Lambda(u)=R\quad\text{for every }u\notin R.
\]

**Status:** **Proved; independently audited (AUD-VC-01).**  This is a genuine
wreath/substitution theorem, but the uniform-interface hypothesis is
essential to this construction.

### VC-04: audited binary-axis theorem

Split the maximal colours as (K=V\sqcup D), with both parts nonempty, and
put

\[
 X=\{p:T(p)\subseteq V\},\quad
 Z=\{p:T(p)\subseteq D\},\quad
 Y=\{p:T(p)\cap V\ne\varnothing\ne T(p)\cap D\}.
\]

The restrictions to the corresponding coordinate blocks show that (X) and
(Z) are arbitrary-base feasible rooted frames.  For (y\in Y), choose
(A) with (f(A)=y), and split (A=A_V\sqcup A_D) by coordinate colour.
Set

\[
 \alpha(y)=f(A_V),\qquad \beta(y)=f(A_D).
\]

These values do not depend on the representative.  Indeed the exact slice
identity gives

\[
 \uparrow\alpha(y)=\{q\ge y:T(q)\subseteq V\},\qquad
 \uparrow\beta(y)=\{q\ge y:T(q)\subseteq D\}. \tag{AX}
\]

Thus (\alpha(y)) and (\beta(y)) are the intrinsic roots of the two pure
slices.  Moreover

\[
 \pi=(\alpha,\beta):Y\twoheadrightarrow_pX\times Z
\]

is an onto p-morphism (where (Y) carries its induced order).  Onto-ness is
obtained by taking disjoint representatives of arbitrary (x\in X,z\in Z)
and forming their union.  For Back, represent (y) on (A), independently
choose (B_V\subseteq A_V) and (B_D\subseteq A_D) for prescribed
(x'\ge\alpha(y)), (z'\ge\beta(y)), and use
(f(B_V\cup B_D)\ge y).

If (\pi) is injective, it is an order isomorphism on the mixed part and the
slice equations (AX) show that the whole frame is the simplicial join

\[
 P\cong X\star Z=X\ \sqcup\ Z\ \sqcup\ (X\times Z).
\]

Consequently, if (M_a\twoheadrightarrow_pX) and
(M_b\twoheadrightarrow_pZ), then (M_{a+b}\twoheadrightarrow_pP): on a
face in the disjoint union of the two coordinate blocks use the first map on
pure first-block faces, the second map on pure second-block faces, and the
ordered pair on mixed faces.

**Status:** **Proved; independently audited (AUD-VC-02).**  Recursively
axis-injective (support-separable) frames therefore fold, with source
dimensions adding down the decomposition tree.  The exact residual datum is
a non-singleton same-axis fibre (\pi^{-1}(x,z)).

### VC-05: greatest axis fibres -- proved boundary case and remaining gap

If every fibre of (\pi:Y\to X\times Z) has a greatest point (t_{x,z}),
then (b\mapsto t_b) is an order-preserving right adjoint section of (\pi):

\[
 \pi(y)\le b\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad y\le t_b.
\]

This does **not** make the section a p-morphism: a non-greatest successor in a
fibre is not hit by the section.  The four-point duplicated crown
(r<s<m,n) is the smallest warning.  Its unique axis fibre is the chain
(r<s), but the greatest-point section sees only (s); one extra coordinate
is needed to realize (r) (and (M_3\twoheadrightarrow_pP)).

A nontrivial boundary case can nevertheless be completed.  Suppose (P) has
exactly two maximal points (m,n), the (n)-pure part is the singleton
(\{n\}), and the mixed part (Y) has a greatest point.  Then (Y) is a
finite rooted frame with a top, so the Maksimova--Skvortsov--Shehtman lemma
gives (k:M_a\twoheadrightarrow_pY).  The first axis map
(\alpha:Y\twoheadrightarrow_pX=P_{\{m\}}) is a p-morphism.  On
(M_{A\sqcup\{e\}}), define

\[
 F(S)=
 \begin{cases}
   \alpha(k(S)),&\varnothing\ne S\subseteq A,\\
   n,&S=\{e\},\\
   k(S\cap A),&e\in S\text{ and }S\cap A\ne\varnothing.
 \end{cases}
\]

For a mixed face, its mixed subfaces give exactly the mixed successors by
Back for (k), its pure (A)-subfaces give exactly
(\uparrow\alpha(k(S\cap A))), and (\{e\}) gives (n).  These are exactly
its three slices.  Pure faces use the p-map (\alpha k).  Hence (F) is an
onto p-morphism.  This proves the greatest-fibre claim when one pure boundary
is trivial.

The general greatest-fibre assertion remains open.  The obstruction is now
precise: for nontrivial (X) and (Z), separate finite realizations give a
map to (X\times Z), but lifting it through (\pi) by the greatest-point
section omits all lower fibre points and is not Back-exact.  Encoding a
realization (M_c\to Y) by pairs of differently coloured coordinates also
fails on unmatched subfaces: diagonal pairs realize the intended lower fibre
points, while a non-diagonal subface need not lie above them.  Thus a proof
needs a **relative two-axis folding lemma**, not merely top points in the
fibres.

Computational evidence supports, but does not prove, the greatest-fibre
conjecture: every rooted target through seven points for which the intrinsic
axis map is an onto p-morphism and all of its fibres have greatest elements
has a finite witness (the eleven seven-point instances first occur in
dimensions (4,4,5,4,5,5,4,5,5,5,6)).

**Status:** right-adjoint structure and the one-trivial-boundary theorem are
**Proved**; the general greatest-fibre folding claim is **Open**.  The branch
has reached the same relative-lifting obstruction as the previous
link-profile approach and is retired after this obstruction report.

### VC-06: single next lemma

The precise remaining statement is the following relative version of finite
folding.

> **Relative two-axis folding lemma.**  Let (P=X\sqcup Z\sqcup Y) be a
> finite supported frame with the pure-slice identities (AX) and an onto
> p-morphism (\pi:Y\twoheadrightarrow_pX\times Z).  If an arbitrary-base
> two-colour face map onto (P) exists and (X,Z) are finite Medvedev
> images, then a finite-base two-colour face map onto (P) exists.

It is enough to prove this lemma with one side a singleton maximal-colour
block.  Indeed, split off one maximal colour and induct on the number of maximal
colours; the base case is the classical top-frame lemma.  The same-axis fibres
are then characterized by their coordinate-replacement graphs and their
higher analogues: the (r)-th profile records all labels obtained after up to
(r) replacements in either colour while the two pure axis labels are held
fixed.  A computable stabilization bound for these profiles yields the finite
map by retaining one bounded family of representatives of each stabilized
role.

The six-point frame 6:43 shows why the full hierarchy, rather than just the
axis fibres or the one-foreign-block law, is necessary.  Its single mixed
axis fibre has a root below three clean crown roots.  All pure slice laws hold,
but the three crown colours would colour the edges of a complete bipartite
graph.  Incident edges of different colours force a three-coordinate face to
the root, whose Back condition asks for the absent third crown colour.
Therefore incident edges have one colour; connectedness makes every edge the
same colour, contradicting onto-ness.  This is the minimal joint
synchronization obstruction detected by the first replacement graph.

**Status:** **Open, sharply isolated.**  This is the single recommended next
lemma; proving it gives full finite folding, whereas refuting it supplies the
desired separation (Y_{ML}\ne ML).

### The explicit stabilization counterexample

Let

\[
 \Omega=\{a_i:i\in\mathbb N\}\sqcup\{b_n:n\ge1\},
 \qquad F_n=\{a_0,\ldots,a_{n-1},b_n\},
\]

and let \(C_2=\{r<t\}\).  Color every nonempty finite or cofinite
\(X\subseteq\Omega\) by \(r\) exactly when \(F_n\subseteq X\) for some
\(n\), and by \(t\) otherwise.  The \(t\)-class is hereditary and contains
every singleton.  Thus a \(t\)-face has cone image \(\{t\}\), while an
\(r\)-face has itself and a \(t\)-singleton as subfaces, so its cone image is
\(\{r,t\}\).  This proves the full p-morphism equation, including at the
cofinite root \(\Omega\).

For an arbitrary finite proposed exceptional set \(H\), take the context

\[
 E=H\cap\{a_i:i\in\mathbb N\},
 \qquad k=\min\{i:a_i\notin H\}.
\]

A sufficiently late \(F_n\) is initially completable from the tail, so
\(r\) occurs in the spectrum over \(E\).  The finite tail deletion

\[
 D=\{a_k\}\cup\{b_i:b_i\notin H,\ 1\le i\le k\}
\]

removes every such completion: \(F_i\) with \(i>k\) needs \(a_k\), while
\(F_i\) with \(i\le k\) needs a \(b_i\) that is either deleted or was omitted
from the chosen context.  Hence no finite \(H\) stabilizes all contexts.  If
each coordinate is replaced by an arbitrary finite nonempty fibre, choose an
untouched \(a_k\)-fibre and delete that whole fibre together with the finitely
many relevant \(b_i\)-fibres; the same proof applies.

This example has deliberately sharp scope.  Its target \(C_2\) folds from
\(M_2\), so it refutes stabilization and occurrence-preserving extraction,
not fresh finite re-realization.

### Exact surviving fixed-\(H\) certificate

For a fixed finite exceptional set \(H\), a finite certificate consists of:

* a finite Medvedev image \(R\);
* a coloring \(\beta:M_H\to P\) of the nonempty pure-exception faces; and
* maps \(\alpha_S:R\to P\) for all \(S\subseteq H\).

They must satisfy

\[
 \{\beta(T):\varnothing\ne T\subseteq S\}=\uparrow\beta(S)
 \qquad(\varnothing\ne S\subseteq H),                         \tag{EA.1}
\]

and

\[
 \uparrow\alpha_S(u)=
 \{\beta(T):\varnothing\ne T\subseteq S\}
 \cup\bigcup_{T\subseteq S}\alpha_T[\uparrow_Ru]
 \qquad(u\in R,\ S\subseteq H),                              \tag{EA.2}
\]

with the displayed images covering \(P\).  Given
\(g:M_J\twoheadrightarrow_pR\), the formula

\[
 G(B\cup S)=
 \begin{cases}
  \alpha_S(g(B)),&B\ne\varnothing,\\
  \beta(S),&B=\varnothing,\ S\ne\varnothing
 \end{cases}                                                  \tag{EA.3}
\]

is then an onto p-morphism.  Conversely, every finite relative realization
supplies (EA.1)--(EA.3), taking \(R=M_J\).  The auditor checked both
directions.  Thus this condition is exact but becomes a theorem only after an
independent small-model bound is proved.

The precise remaining positive target is:

> **Bounded cubical/simplicial certificate condition.**  Produce a computable
> \(B(P,H)\) such that arbitrary finite/cofinite \(H\)-context feasibility
> implies a certificate (EA.1)--(EA.2) with \(R\) covered by some \(M_j\),
> \(j\le B(P,H)\); then prove the corresponding bound uniformly through the
> successive block interfaces of the four-end frame.

Fixed \(H\) alone is not yet the four-end theorem: the other finite/cofinite
blocks still provide infinite context.  A full positive solution must either
iterate the certificate theorem uniformly through those blocks or replace
\(B(P,H)\) by a target-only bound that eliminates the unbounded-context
parameter.

### Algebraic form of the same endpoint

Writing \(H_P=\operatorname{Up}(P)\), an exceptional witness is an embedding
\(H_P\hookrightarrow E(A)\) encoded by Heyting homomorphisms
\(\alpha:H_P\to A\), \(\epsilon:H_P\to2\), and a bounded-distributive-lattice
homomorphism \(\beta:H_P\to A\).  The latter obeys

\[
 \beta(x\to y)=
 \begin{cases}
 (\alpha x\to\alpha y)\wedge(\beta x\to\beta y),
       &\epsilon x\le\epsilon y,\\
 0,    &\epsilon x=1,\ \epsilon y=0.
 \end{cases}                                                  \tag{EA.4}
\]

The old quotient shows only that \(\alpha[H_P]\) is \(V_M\)-exact.  Since
\(H_P\) is subdirectly irreducible, the exact algebraic residual is to find a
finite \(A_n\)-valued solution of (EA.4) whose combined map separates one
generating pair of the monolith.  That is equivalent to an embedding
\(H_P\hookrightarrow E(A_n)=A_{n+1}\), and hence to folding.  Grigolia's
projectivity of the \(A_n\) does not produce this solution: it operates inside
\(V_M\), while membership of \(H_P\) in \(V_M\) is precisely what is unknown.
